# Editorial Standards

## Core rule

Be forceful about the argument and precise about the evidence.

## Creator-led drafting

The creator owns the thesis, taste and emotional direction. Begin with their take and build its strongest honest version. Do not replace it with a neutral summary or the most common online opinion. A source may correct a factual premise; it does not decide the creator's values or taste.

Draft before all research is complete. Keep unresolved facts in production notes with claim IDs and explicit placeholders. Only the affected factual passage remains unlocked; do not block the whole story. An opinion label does not make a false factual premise acceptable.

Keep routine evidence labels, counterevidence and clearance notes off the spoken track. Say a qualification aloud when omitting it would materially mislead, especially about intent or wrongdoing. Present the strongest relevant counterargument once at the point it matters; avoid repeating a disclaimer after every provocative sentence.

When evidence conflicts with the creator's take, report the exact conflict and offer the closest supportable wording. Never quietly change the thesis, invent support, or hide decisive counterevidence. Distinguish taste ("I find this narrative exhausting"), interpretation ("My reading is that marketing shaped how this was sold") and an allegation of conduct ("officials were instructed to help"). The last requires attribution and appropriate evidence, even when prefaced with "I think."

Script review must check both factual accuracy and whether the rewrite still expresses the creator's intended argument.

The channel may say:

- "This incident fueled allegations that the tournament favored Argentina."
- "Critics interpret this sequence as evidence of institutional favoritism."
- "The available footage supports a foul argument, but does not itself prove a coordinated fix."

The channel should not say, without compelling evidence:

- "FIFA rigged the tournament."
- "Infantino ordered officials to help Messi."
- "Businesses forced Messi to return."

## Source hierarchy

1. Full match footage, official competition records, laws of the game, disciplinary decisions
2. Direct statements and complete interviews
3. Reputable investigative or contemporaneous reporting
4. Qualified referee/technical analysis with disclosed reasoning
5. Academic or industry material
6. Fan analysis, social media, commentary videos

Levels 4-6 may support interpretation or sentiment. They cannot independently prove institutional corruption.

## Claim record requirements

Each consequential claim must record:

- Exact wording proposed for narration
- Internal evidence label
- Event date and publication date
- Primary source URL/file
- Supporting source URL/file
- Best counterevidence
- Confidence level
- Permission/copyright notes for any media
- Final editorial decision

## Fairness test

Before publication, ask:

1. What is the strongest innocent explanation?
2. Are chronology and causation being confused?
3. Was the same officiating standard applied elsewhere?
4. Is a cropped or slow-motion angle misleading?
5. Would the wording survive if the subject watched the full video?
6. Does the title promise more than the evidence delivers?

## Media-use policy

- Prefer original diagrams, Blender recreations, licensed stills, and short transformative excerpts where lawful.
- Public availability does not grant reuse rights.
- Preserve source and license information for every external asset.
- Do not reproduce another creator's script, distinctive sequence, music, or visual identity.
